# Simulated interview — Business User

> **Simulated persona-based research sessions used to approximate likely interview findings for the purpose of the hackathon.** Answers are modeled from the supplied persona, not quotes from a real person.

**Profile assumption:** operations coordinator installing the product on a company laptop without routine terminal use.

**What do Python and CUDA mean to you?** “I recognize Python as programming. CUDA means nothing. If either is required, tell me whether my computer is okay—not give me a lesson.”

**What should happen when Python is missing?** “Fix it for me if that is safe.” Later, when told a fix may require administrator approval: “Then don’t start changing things—tell me who to contact.” This contradicts the initial preference for total automation.

**How do you react to terminal output?** “It looks like something has broken even when it is working. I’d close it.” They still want a “details” link to copy information for support.

**What progress is helpful?** Named steps and an honest time/size estimate. A spinner without explanation feels frozen.

**What about microphone permission?** Wants the reason before the OS prompt and reassurance that audio is not being recorded during setup.

**How should failure work?** Keep completed work; recommend one safe action; make “Try again” obvious. They do not want to repeat choices.

**What proves success?** Launch the app and confirm microphone/model checks, not merely “files copied.” Wants a single “Open LITIT AI Desktop” action.

**Synthesis note:** default to plain language and recommendation, but preserve support-ready evidence on demand. **Future validation requirement:** test whether remediation wording creates informed consent rather than blind acceptance.
