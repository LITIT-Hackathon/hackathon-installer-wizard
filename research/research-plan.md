# Research plan

> **Simulated persona-based research sessions used to approximate likely interview findings for the purpose of the hackathon.** No real human interviews were conducted.

## Objective

Understand how the supplied personas judge compatibility, trust installer changes, recover from failure, and balance automation with control. Use findings to prioritize a demonstrable 24-hour experience.

## Questions

1. What information creates confidence before installation?
2. Which terminology and diagnostics are useful or intimidating?
3. Which changes may be automated, and which require consent?
4. What does recoverable failure mean to each persona?
5. What proof is required at completion?

## Method

Four 30-minute simulated, semi-structured sessions grounded only in supplied persona traits, followed by affinity synthesis and a traceability review against the reference product. Scenarios A–E are used as prompts. Contradictions are retained rather than averaged away.

## Participants

One modeled session each for Business User, Developer, Enterprise IT Administrator, and ML Engineer. **Assumption:** contextual details added to make answers concrete are plausible, not factual user evidence.

## Analysis and ethics

Responses are coded by comprehension, control, trust, failure recovery, diagnostics, and enterprise constraints. Findings carry confidence based on source support: high = directly supported by repository persona/product; medium = repeated across simulations or strongly implied; low = single simulated preference. These artifacts must never be presented as validated customer research.

## Success criteria

- At least one actionable need per persona.
- Contradictions explicitly influence progressive disclosure and configuration choices.
- P0 design decisions trace to evidence and testable acceptance criteria.

## Future validation requirements

Recruit real representatives; observe installations on managed and unmanaged Windows/macOS/Linux machines; test comprehension of “compatibility,” “remediation,” and CUDA choices; measure task completion, recovery rate, time, and confidence; validate screen-reader and keyboard use.
